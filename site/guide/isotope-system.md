# Isotope System

Isotope System is the window that manages the computer. It is on the four desktops.

## Open Isotope System

Do one of these:

- Press [[Super+Escape]] and choose **System**.
- Open it from the list of your apps. Its name is **Isotope System**.
- In a terminal, type `isotope system`. To open one page, add its name, for example `isotope system network`.

The pages are in the list at the left of the window. When the window is narrow, the list is hidden: click the button at the top left to show it.

Each page shows its items as rows, with their buttons at the right. When a row has more buttons than fit, and for the buttons that delete something, click **⋯** at the right of the row to see them.

## Who can change what

Every user can open Isotope System. Most changes to the system need an administrator: a user in the `admin` group. The account that the installer makes is an administrator. If you are not an administrator, the page tells you what you cannot do.

These changes are open to every user:

- Install, update and remove your own apps.
- Connect to Wi-Fi networks and VPNs.
- Pair Bluetooth devices.
- Mount USB disks and SMB network shares.
- Print a test page.
- Change your own password.

## The About page

The **About** page shows:

- The version of Isotope and of Hadron, the system that Isotope is built on.
- The desktop, the image (hardware or virtual machine) and the edition.
- The kernel and the computer name.
- The graphics: the graphics card that draws the screen, or **Software** with the reason.

Click **What's new in this release** to read the release notes. Click **User guide** to open this guide.

## On the live desktop

On the live desktop, the pages that need an installed system say **Not available on the live system. Install Isotope first.** These pages are Apps, Updates, Desktop & image, Extensions, Backup, Security, Users and Compatibility. The other pages work.
